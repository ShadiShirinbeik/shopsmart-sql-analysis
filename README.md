# ShopSmart · SQL Sales Analysis

An end-to-end SQL analysis of a small e-commerce dataset in **PostgreSQL**: 200 customers, 50 products in 5 categories, 300 orders (Oct 2024 – Oct 2025), 970 order lines and 20 time-boxed promotions.

The business team asked three things: *how do our customers behave, which categories perform, and do promotions actually work?* This repo answers those questions with 20 SQL queries and documents what each one found.

**[→ Read the queries](sql/03_analysis.sql)** · [→ Summary page](https://shadishirinbeik.github.io/shopsmart-sql-analysis/) (charts of the query results, generated with AI assistance — see note below)

## Key findings

**Customers**
- Customers are spread almost evenly across the four regions (43–57 each), but their spending is not. West customers place the biggest orders (about \$2,567 on average); East customers place the smallest (about \$1,662). North and South passed the \$175k sales target, East is far behind at about \$110k. East has both the fewest big orders and the lowest total.
- Repeat purchasing is rare. In the last 6 months of data only one customer ordered more than 3 times, and 81 of 115 active customers placed just one order. There is no real "loyal core" yet.
- 74 of 159 buyers have an average order value above the company average (~\$2,161), but most of them got there with a single large purchase, not with regular big orders.
- Customers shop widely: 71 of 159 buyers (45 %) have bought from 4 or 5 of the 5 categories, and only 15 stick to a single category. 79 of 159 buyers (half) have bought both Electronics and Sports products.

**Categories**
- Electronics is the strongest category on every measure: highest average list price (~\$280), most revenue, and the only category that passes \$20k in all four regions in the last 6 months (Electronics–South is the strongest pair at ~\$34k). Sports clears \$20k only in South and North, Beauty only in North; Clothing and Home reach it nowhere.
- By volume, Sports leads: the best-selling product is "How" (Sports, 83 units) and Sports has 3 of the top 4 products.
- Electronics also has the widest reach: 119 of 200 customers (60 %) have bought from it at least once, followed by Clothing (113) and Sports (109). Home is the weakest category: only 4 products, the lowest revenue, and only 60 customers (30 %) have ever bought from it.
- Every product in the catalog has sold at least once, so there is no dead stock to remove.
- Discounts are applied uniformly, not strategically. No category has an average line discount above 10 %; all five sit between 7.5 % and 8.0 %.

**Promotions**
- Promotions on "How" (Sports) generated by far the most revenue (~\$22.7k, ~\$18k and ~\$8.5k), but three overlapping promotions on the same product make it impossible to credit any single one. Together they earned more than the other 17 promotions combined.
- Two promotions sold nothing at all. The shortest one ran for only 7 days.
- A promotion did not make a product a top seller: 8 of the 17 promoted products still sold less than the average product in their category. Four of them are more than 55 % below that average.
- The most discounted product in each category (by total discount amount) is simply its best seller — "How" in Sports, "Foot" in Electronics, "Term" in Beauty — because discounts are flat across products, so the biggest seller collects the biggest discount total.
- Cameron Bradley (~\$2,840) and Nicholas Johnson (~\$2,600) spent the most on promoted products while a promotion was active.

**Trend**
- Monthly revenue goes up and down a lot, between about \$34k and \$70k, with no clear seasonal pattern. September 2025 is the peak. October 2024 and October 2025 are partial months and are excluded from comparisons.
- Average order value grew from one month to the next in 22 of 48 region-months. West grew most often (7 of 12 months), East least often (4 of 12).

## Data model

```
customers ──< orders ──< order_items >── products ──< promotions
 (200)        (300)        (970)          (50)          (20)
```

| Table | Grain | Primary key | Foreign keys |
|---|---|---|---|
| `customers` | one customer | `customer_id` | — |
| `products` | one catalog product | `product_id` | — |
| `orders` | one order (header) | `order_id` | `customer_id → customers` |
| `order_items` | one product line inside an order | `order_item_id` | `order_id → orders`, `product_id → products` |
| `promotions` | one time-boxed discount on one product | `promotion_id` | `product_id → products` |

`order_items.unit_price` is the price at the time of sale, kept separately from `products.unit_price` (the catalog price) so that historical orders do not change when the catalog is repriced. `order_items.discount` is a fraction (0.10 = 10 %).

Schema with constraints: [`sql/01_schema.sql`](sql/01_schema.sql).

## How to reproduce

Requires PostgreSQL (tested on 16). From the repo root:

```bash
createdb shopsmart
psql -d shopsmart -f sql/01_schema.sql   # tables, PKs, FKs
psql -d shopsmart -f sql/02_load.sql     # \copy the CSVs in data/ and print row counts
psql -d shopsmart -f sql/03_analysis.sql # the 20 analysis queries
```

Prefer a GUI? Run `01_schema.sql` in DBeaver / pgAdmin, then import the five CSVs from `data/` into the matching tables — parents first (`customers`, `products`, `orders`, `promotions`, `order_items`).

Expected row counts after loading: 200 / 50 / 300 / 20 / 970.

## The 20 business questions

| # | Topic | Question | Technique |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customer segmentation | Customers per region | `GROUP BY` |
| 2 | Regional performance | Average order value per region | `JOIN` + `AVG` |
| 3 | Product mix | Average list price per category | `GROUP BY` |
| 4 | Revenue trend | Revenue per month | `DATE_TRUNC` |
| 5 | Product performance | Top 10 products by units | `SUM` + `LIMIT` |
| 6 | Loyalty | Customers with > 3 orders in the last 6 months of data | scalar subquery, `HAVING` |
| 7 | Pricing | Categories with avg line discount > 10 % | `HAVING` on `AVG` |
| 8 | Regional targets | Regions above \$175k sales | `HAVING` on `SUM` |
| 9 | High-value customers | Customers whose AOV beats the overall AOV | CTE + scalar subquery |
| 10 | Catalog health | Products never sold | `LEFT JOIN … IS NULL` |
| 11 | Regional sales by category | Category × region net revenue, last 6 months, > \$20k | 4-table join, net-revenue formula |
| 12 | Cross-category buyers | Customers buying from > 3 categories | `COUNT(DISTINCT)` |
| 13 | Discount depth | Avg discount per category with ≥ 50 units | `HAVING`, `FILTER` |
| 14 | Campaign response | Top 5 spenders during active promotions | `EXISTS` on a date range |
| 15 | Promotion revenue | Revenue per promotion inside its window | `LEFT JOIN` with range condition, `COALESCE` |
| 16 | Underperforming promotions | Promoted products below category-average sales | two CTEs, `EXISTS` |
| 17 | Month-over-month growth | Region-months where AOV grew | self-join on `month − 1` (no window functions) |
| 18 | Market basket | Customers who bought Electronics *and* Sports | conditional aggregation |
| 19 | Market penetration | Unique buyers per category, ≥ 40 | `COUNT(DISTINCT)` |
| 20 | Discount concentration | Most-discounted product per category | correlated subquery (no window functions) |

Every query in [`sql/03_analysis.sql`](sql/03_analysis.sql) is preceded by the business question and followed by a one-line insight.
